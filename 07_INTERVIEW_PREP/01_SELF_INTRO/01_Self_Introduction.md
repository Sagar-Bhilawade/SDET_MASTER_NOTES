# Self-Introduction — The 60-Second Opener

## What it is

The answer to *"Tell me about yourself"* — the first question in almost every round, and the one that sets the frame for everything after. It is not your CV read aloud. It is a 60-second pitch that lands three things: who you are now, the shape of your experience, and why you're in this room.

---

## Why it matters

This is the only answer you can fully script and rehearse, because it's asked every single time. It's also the highest-leverage 60 seconds in the interview: a crisp opener makes the interviewer relax and follow your lead into your strengths; a rambling one makes them start probing to find the real story. Most candidates narrate chronologically from college and lose the room by the third sentence. You will not.

The rule: **professional only, present-first, ending on why this role.** Nothing about civil engineering, PG-DAC, or how you got into testing unless they ask — that's HR-round material, not the opener.

---

## 30-second answer (the core)

> "I'm an SDET with 4+ years at NielsenIQ across manual and automation testing. My current track is Playwright with TypeScript, where I work on UI E2E automation with API-assisted setup and cleanup, reusable fixtures, CI execution, and AI-assisted QA workflows. Before that I spent around two years on an enterprise Selenium and Java stack with RestAssured API validation, SQL checks, and Jenkins pipelines. I'm ISTQB CTFL and Azure AZ-900 certified, and I'm looking for an SDET role where I can own more of the automation strategy and quality engineering approach."

That's it. Memorize the shape, not the words.

## 2-minute answer (when they want more)

Same spine, with one metric and one signature story hook added:

> "I'm an SDET with 4+ years at NielsenIQ across manual and automation testing. My work splits into two tracks. The current one — since the start of 2025 — is a modern Playwright and TypeScript framework where I work on UI E2E automation, API-assisted test data setup and cleanup, reusable fixtures, and CI-ready execution. The headline result there was cutting the CI feedback loop from about 40 minutes down to 12, mostly through better parallel execution and removing wasted waits.
>
> Before that, my first two years were an enterprise Selenium and Java stack — 300-plus automated scenarios, RestAssured for the API layer, SQL for database validation, Jenkins pipelines. That's where I did the hard flakiness work and got the suite under 2% flaky on first attempt.
>
> I'm ISTQB CTFL and Azure AZ-900 certified, and I use AI-assisted workflows — GitHub Copilot Chat and Playwright CLI workflows — practically, for test drafting, locator refinement, and maintenance, which has been about a 30% efficiency gain.
>
> Having built in both a legacy and a modern paradigm is really what shapes how I make tooling decisions now. What I'm after next is a role with more ownership on the architecture and strategy side."

---

## Why each piece is there

| Piece | Why it's in the opener |
|-------|------------------------|
| "4+ years" | Establishes seniority band up front |
| Playwright **first** | Leads with the in-demand skill; mirrors most JDs |
| "own framework design" | Signals you're not just a test-writer — the 20 LPA line |
| One metric (40→12 or <2%) | Concrete, memorable, verifiable against the resume |
| Selenium **second, briefly** | Depth and range without diluting the Playwright lead |
| "ISTQB and Azure" | Formal credibility, one clause, then move on |
| "not just implementation" | Names the reason you're switching — forward-looking |

## Common interview Q&A

**Q: "Tell me about yourself."**
The 30- or 60-second version above. Read the room — if it's a screening call, keep it to 30 seconds and let them pull.

**Q: "Walk me through your background."**
Slightly more permission to go chronological, but still lead present-first. Two-track story, then one line on certifications. Still no personal history unless asked.

**Q: "Why are you not in development, given your background?"** *(if the pivot comes up)*
Don't get defensive. *"I came into testing early and found I was strong at the engineering side of it — framework design, CI, the systems thinking. SDET is where development and testing meet, and that's genuinely where I do my best work."* Then steer back to the two-track story.

**Q: "What do you do day-to-day?"**
Concrete: *"Own and extend the Playwright framework, write and review UI and API tests, keep the CI pipeline healthy, and unblock the team on flaky or slow tests. I'm the person who gets pulled in when something's red and nobody knows why."*

## Weak areas to watch

- **Don't narrate your CV chronologically from college.** The single most common mistake. Present-first, always.
- **Don't list tools like a skills dump.** "Playwright, Selenium, Java, TypeScript, Jenkins, Azure, Docker, K8s..." is noise. Two tracks, one metric each.
- **Don't undersell the ownership.** "I write automation tests" is a 13 LPA sentence. "I own framework design" is the 20 LPA version — and it's true.
- **Don't run past 90 seconds.** If you're still talking at two minutes with no question, you've lost them. End on "why this role" and stop.
- **Keep it strictly professional and SDET-focused.** Do not add unrelated personal or financial context.
- **Match the metric to the track.** 40→12 is Playwright. Under 2% flaky is Selenium/CIP. Don't cross them — the resume is open in front of them.

---

## Key takeaway

> **Present-first, two tracks, one metric, end on why you're here.** The opener isn't your history — it's the frame you want the interviewer to think inside for the next hour. Lead with Playwright and framework ownership, because that's both the market's demand and your genuine strength.
